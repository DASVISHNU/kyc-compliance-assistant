p3 | Introduction
p3 | In order to prevent banks and other financial institutions from being used as a channel
p3 | for Money Laundering (ML) / Terrorist Financing (TF) and to ensure the integrity and
p3 | stability of the financial system, efforts are continuously being made both
p3 | internationally and nationally, by way of prescribing various rules and regulations.
p3 | Internationally, the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), which is an inter-governmental
p3 | body established in 1989 by the Ministers of its member jurisdictions, sets standards
p3 | and promotes effective implementation of legal, regulatory and operational measures
p3 | for combating money laundering, terrorist financing and other related threats to the
p3 | integrity of the international financial system. India, as a member of FATF, is committed
p3 | to upholding measures to protect the integrity of the international financial system.
p3 | In India, the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002, and the Prevention of Money-
p3 | Laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005, form the legal framework on Anti-
p3 | Money Laundering (AML) and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (CFT). The
p3 | provisions of the PML Act, 2002 and the PML Rules, 2005, as amended from time to
p3 | time by the Government of India, require Regulated Entities (REs) to follow certain
p3 | customer identification procedures while undertaking a transaction either by
p3 | establishing an account -based relationship or otherwise, and to monitor their
p3 | transactions.
p3 | Accordingly, in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 35A of the Banking
p3 | Regulation Act, 1949, section 10(2) read with section 18 of Payment and Settlement
p3 | Systems Act 2007 (Act 51 of 2007), section 11(1) of the Foreign Exchange Management
p3 | Act, 1999, Rule 9(14) of the Prevention of Money -Laundering (Maintenance of
p3 | Records) Rules, 2005, and all other laws enabling the Reserve Bank in this regard, the
p3 | RBI being satisfied that it is necessary and expedient in the public interest so to do ,
p3 | hereby issues the Directions hereinafter specified.
p4 | Chapter I – Preliminary
p4 | A. Short Title and Commencement.
p4 | 1. These Directions shall be called the Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks –
p4 | Know Your Customer) Directions, 2025.
p4 | 2. These directions shall come into effect on the day they are placed on the official
p4 | website of the RBI.
p4 | B. Applicability
p4 | 3. These directions shall be applicable to Commercial Banks (hereinafter collectively
p4 | referred to as 'banks' and individually as a 'bank').
p4 | For the purpose of these Directions, ‘Commercial Banks’ mean banking companies
p4 | (other than Small Finance Banks, Payment Banks, and Local Area Banks),
p4 | corresponding new banks, and the State Bank of India, as defined respectively under
p4 | clauses (c), (da), and (nc) of Section 5 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
p4 | 4. These directions shall also apply to those branches and majority-owned subsidiaries
p4 | of the b ank which are located abroad, to the extent they are not contradictory to the
p4 | local laws in the host country, provided that:
p4 | (1) where applicable laws and regulations prohibit implementation of these guidelines,
p4 | the bank shall bring the same to the notice of the RBI. The RBI may advise the bank
p4 | to take further necessary action, including application of additional measures to
p4 | manage the ML / TF risks.
p4 | (2) in case there is a variance in KYC / AML standards prescribed by the RBI and the
p4 | host country regulators, branches / subsidiaries of the bank shall adopt the more
p4 | stringent regulation of the two.
p4 | (3) branches/ subsidiaries of a Foreign Incorporated bank may adopt the more
p4 | stringent regulation of the two i.e., standards prescribed by RBI and their home country
p4 | regulators.
p4 | Provided that this rule shall not apply to ‘small accounts’ referred to in paragraph 28 of
p4 | Chapter VI.

p16 | Chapter II – General
p16 | A. Board of Directors / Policies related guidelines:
p16 | 6. Know Your Customer (KYC) Policy:
p16 | (1) The bank shall have a KYC policy. The Board of Directors of the bank, or any
p16 | committee to which the Board has delegated power, shall duly approve the KYC policy.
p16 | (2) The KYC policy shall include following four key elements:
p16 | (i) Customer Acceptance Policy;
p16 | (ii) Risk Management
p16 | (iii) Customer Identification Procedures (CIP); and
p16 | (iv) Monitoring of Transactions
p16 | (3) The KYC policy shall, inter alia include:
p16 | (i) Periodic updation of KYC
p16 | (ii) Any exceptional measures for KYC updation, such as requiring a recent
p16 | photograph, physical presence, or a more frequent updation schedule than the
p16 | minimum prescribed.
p16 | (iii) Obtaining a copy of OVD or deemed OVD, for the purpose of proof of change
p16 | of address during KYC updation.
p16 | (iv) Providing facility of updation / periodic updation of KYC at any branch.
p16 | (4) Furthermore, the bank shall have the following:
p16 | (i) Policies approved by the Board detailing a robust due diligence process for
p16 | managing request s to change the registered Mobile Number for Accounts
p16 | opened in non-face-to-face mode.
p16 | (ii) Policy approved by the Board or a committee headed by the Chairman / CEO /
p16 | MD, to establish parameters for approving cross-border correspondent banking
p16 | relationships. New correspondent banking relationships require post facto
p16 | approval from the Board or the empowered committee.
p16 | 7. The specific responsibilities and actions stipulated for the Board or its relevant
p16 | committees in paragraph 6 above are elaborated upon in greater detail within the
p17 | Directions.
p17 | 8. In terms of PML Rules, groups shall implement group-wide policies for the purpose
p17 | of discharging obligations under the provisions of Chapter IV of the PML Act, 2002. (15
p17 | of 2003). Accordingly, every bank which is part of a group, shall implement group-wide
p17 | programmes against money laundering and terror financing, including group- wide
p17 | policies for sharing information required for the purposes of client due diligence, money
p17 | laundering, and terrorist finance risk management, and such programmes shall include
p17 | adequate safeguards on the confidentiality and use of information exchanged,
p17 | including safeguards to prevent tipping-off.
p17 | 9. Bank’s policy framework shall seek to ensure compliance with PML Act / Rules,
p17 | including regulatory instructions in this regard and shall provide a bulwark against
p17 | threats arising from money laundering, terrorist financing, proliferation financing and
p17 | other related risks. While ensuring compliance with the legal / regulatory requirements
p17 | as above, the bank may also consider adoption of best international practices taking
p17 | into account the FATF standards and FATF guidance notes, for managing risks better.
p17 | 10. Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Risk Assessment by the bank:
p17 | (1) The bank shall carry out ‘Money Laundering (ML) and Terrorist Financing (TF) Risk
p17 | Assessment’ exercises periodically to identify, assess and take effective measures to
p17 | mitigate its money laundering and terrorist financing risk for clients, countries or
p17 | geographic areas, products, services, transactions or delivery channels, etc. The
p17 | assessment process shall consider all the relevant risk factors before determining the
p17 | level of overall risk and the appropriate level and type of mitigation to be applied. While
p17 | preparing the internal risk assessment, the bank shall take cognizance of the overall
p17 | sector-specific vulnerabilities, if any, that the regulator / supervisor may share with the

p66 | 74. Wire Transfer
p66 | (1) Information requirements for wire transfers for the purpose of this Direction:
p66 | (i) All cross-border wire transfers shall be accompanied by accurate, complete, and

p66 | (a) name of the originator;

p66 | transaction;

p66 | transaction.

p66 | which permits traceability of the transaction.
p66 | (ii) In case of batch transfer, where several individual cross-border wire transfers from
p66 | a single originator are bundled in a batch file for transmission to beneficiaries, they
p66 | (i.e., individual transfers) are exempted from the requirements of clause (i) above in
p66 | respect of originator information, provided that they include the originator’s account
p66 | number or unique transaction reference number, as mentioned above, and the batch

p66 | information, that is fully traceable within the beneficiary country.
p66 | (iii) Domestic wire transfer, where the originator is an account holder of the ordering
p66 | bank, shall be accompanied by originator and beneficiary information, as indicated for
p67 | cross-border wire transfers in (i) and (ii) above.

p67 | not an account holder of the ordering banks and where the information accompanying
p67 | the wire transfer can be made available to the beneficiary RE and appropriate

p68 | of the bank to comply with applicable reporting requirements under PML Act, 2002,
